Yes—what you’ve sketched *does* read like a progressive compression of degrees of freedom, but it’s not just “less freedom”; it’s a **structured projection** across levels.

Think of it less as loss and more as **aggregation + constraint**:

* **Tensor → Matrix (Person → Kin)**
  A person carries many independent axes (beliefs, skills, states). Enter a network, and those axes get **coupled**—relationships impose correlations. You move from full multidimensional freedom to **relational structure**.

* **Matrix → Vector (Kin → Ecosystem)**
  The network collapses into **flows**—resources, information, energy. You don’t track every relationship anymore; you track **net direction and magnitude**. That’s a projection onto fewer dimensions.

* **Vector → Eigenmode (Ecosystem → Federated regulators)**
  Now you’re not even tracking arbitrary flows—you’re extracting the **dominant modes**. Regulation operates on *patterns that persist*: principal components, stable attractors. This is a **basis change + dimensionality reduction**.

* **Eigenmode → Scalar (Civilizational planner)**
  At the top, everything compresses into **objective functions**: GDP, stability, survival probability, “public good.” Multiple modes get reduced to **single metrics or a small set of scalars**—decision criteria.

So yes:
👉 **Degrees of freedom decrease**, but
👉 **Constraint coherence increases**.

You’re essentially describing a pipeline like:

> high-dimensional state space → correlated structure → net flow → dominant modes → objective scalar

In more formal language, it resembles:

* **Coarse-graining** in statistical mechanics
* **Renormalization** (collapsing microstates into macrostates)
* **PCA / spectral decomposition** (extracting eigenmodes)
* **Control theory** (reducing system state to cost functions)

But here’s the tension you’re implicitly pointing at:

**Compression trades fidelity for governability.**

* At the **tensor level**, you have richness but chaos.
* At the **scalar level**, you have clarity but risk *oversimplification*.

The real question isn’t whether compression happens—it must—but:

> **Where do you stop compressing before you destroy the signal you actually care about?**

Because:

* Over-compression → tyranny of metrics (Goodhart’s Law)
* Under-compression → paralysis (too many degrees of freedom)

Your stack is basically a **multi-scale control architecture**. The interesting move would be to make it *bidirectional*:

* Upward: compress (sense → summarize)
* Downward: expand (policy → reconstruct actionable structure)

That closes the loop:

> Scalar → Eigenmode → Vector → Matrix → Tensor
> (policy → regulation → flows → relationships → individual behavior)

If you wire that correctly, you don’t just have compression—you have a **reversible hierarchy of representations**.

Do you want to formalize this as:

* a **category-theoretic mapping** (functors between levels),
